What’s a WhereCamp?

A day where the attendees are the program.

WhereCamp started in 2007 as a free, BarCamp-style companion to O’Reilly’s Where 2.0 conference. Back then, Google Maps mashups, OpenStreetMap, GeoRSS and GPS phones were changing who got to make maps. Geo-enthusiasts, developers, social place hackers, artists and activists showed up with no agenda and built one on a wall of sticky notes.

The format still works. Show up in the morning, pitch a session, vote with your feet. Anyone can lead a session. Demos beat slides. The hallway track is the track.

Mapping has never mattered more: climate adaptation, open data, AI that reasons about place, community and humanitarian mapping, transit, privacy. It’s time to get the community back in a room.

    A note on Null Island

    Null Island is the point at 0°N, 0°E in the Gulf of Guinea, where the Equator meets the Prime Meridian. Nothing is there except a weather buoy (station 13010, “Soul”). Even so, it’s one of the most visited places in GIS: every time a geocoder fails and returns (0, 0), another record washes up on its shores. It’s our patron non-place, and the home of WhereCamp Null Island, the online edition everyone can attend.

    Host a WhereCamp

    All you really need is a room and some sticky notes.

    WhereCamps are independently organised. There’s no franchise fee and no permission needed. Just keep it open, free or cheap, and participant-driven.

    1. A venue for 30–50 people, with a big room plus a couple of breakout spaces. Universities, libraries and friendly offices are classic hosts.
    2. Wi-Fi and power for everyone.
    3. Sticky notes, markers and a wall for the session board.
    4. Coffee and lunch. Local sponsors are usually happy to help.
    5. A date and a few co-organisers. Check the proposals to find people near you.

    Get your camp on the map

    1. Find or open a proposal for your city and rally co-organisers there.
    2. Once you have a date and venue, add a feature to wherecamps.geojson with eventName, startDate, endDate, venue and url, then open a pull request.
    3. It shows up here as upcoming automatically, and moves to the archive once the date has passed.
